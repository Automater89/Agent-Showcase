# Problem Statement: Employee Offboarding Process Redesign

**Prepared by:** AI Process Innovation Analyst  
**Presented to:** Hiring Manager, Human Resources & Operations  
**Organization:** [Automotive Manufacturing Company]  
**Date:** May 2026  
**Project Classification:** Process Redesign Initiative

---

## Background

As a leading automotive manufacturer operating across multiple production facilities, the organization employs thousands of individuals spanning plant-floor technicians, quality engineers, supply chain coordinators, and corporate staff. Employee turnover — whether voluntary, involuntary, or driven by workforce restructuring — is an unavoidable operational reality in this industry.

Despite significant investment in onboarding infrastructure and talent acquisition, the employee **offboarding process** has remained largely manual, inconsistent, and decentralized. As competitive pressures in the automotive sector intensify — driven by the shift to electric vehicle (EV) production, global supply chain volatility, and evolving labor agreements — the cost of a broken offboarding process has grown from a background annoyance into a measurable operational risk.

This document presents a formal problem statement to guide a structured process redesign effort aimed at transforming offboarding from a liability into a controlled, dignified, and data-informed transition experience.

---

## Problem Summary

The current employee offboarding process is fragmented across HR, IT, Payroll, Plant Operations, and Security — with no single owner, no standardized workflow, and no reliable audit trail. Employees exit the organization without consistent knowledge transfer, system access is not revoked in a timely manner, and the experience leaves both departing employees and their former colleagues without the support they need to transition effectively.

The result is a process that generates compliance exposure, institutional knowledge loss, security vulnerabilities, and reputational risk — all of which undermine the organization's operational resilience and employer brand.

## Current Pain Points

- **No standardized offboarding checklist or workflow.** Each department follows its own informal practices, resulting in critical steps — such as system access removal, badge deactivation, or final timesheet approvals — being missed or completed out of sequence.

- **Delayed IT access revocation.** On average, departed employees retain active system credentials for days or weeks post-separation, creating significant cybersecurity and data integrity risks — particularly concerning given the proprietary nature of vehicle design, supply chain, and production data.

- **Knowledge transfer is unstructured and inconsistently executed.** Institutional knowledge — from supplier relationships to machine calibration procedures — frequently walks out the door with the departing employee, with no formal documentation or transition plan in place.

- **Exit interview data is collected but not analyzed.** While some HR teams conduct exit interviews, responses are stored inconsistently and rarely surfaced to leadership in a format that enables trend analysis or systemic improvement.

- **Payroll and benefits errors cause post-separation disputes.** Miscommunication between HR and Payroll about final pay, unused PTO payouts, and benefit continuation timelines results in errors that generate legal risk and damage the organization's reputation as an employer.

- **No visibility into offboarding status across the organization.** HR leadership and plant managers cannot easily determine where any given offboarding stands at a given moment, making escalation, compliance reporting, and backfill planning reactive rather than proactive.

- **The departing employee experience is inconsistent and often impersonal.** Employees who leave — voluntarily or otherwise — frequently report feeling confused, unsupported, or disrespected during their final weeks. This directly affects employer brand, alumni referrals, and potential boomerang hiring in a talent-scarce manufacturing labor market.

## Scope

This process redesign initiative will focus on the following areas:

- **End-to-end offboarding workflow design** — from the moment a separation is confirmed (resignation received or termination decision made) through the employee's final day and 30-day post-separation follow-up.
- **Cross-functional coordination framework** — defining clear ownership, handoff triggers, and SLAs between HR, IT, Payroll, Legal, and Operations.
- **Checklist and automation opportunities** — identifying manual tasks that can be standardized via checklist, workflow tooling, or HRIS automation.
- **Knowledge transfer protocols** — designing a repeatable approach to capturing role-specific knowledge before departure.
- **Exit interview process and data utilization** — standardizing collection and creating a reporting mechanism to surface actionable insights to leadership.
- **Compliance documentation standards** — ensuring all required records are captured, retained, and accessible for audit purposes.
- **Metrics and KPIs** — establishing baseline measurements and targets for offboarding cycle time, access revocation timeliness, error rate, and employee experience scores.

The scope covers **all employee types** across **all facilities**, including salaried, hourly, union-represented, and contract-to-hire roles where applicable.

---

## Out of Scope

The following items are explicitly excluded from this initiative to maintain focus and ensure timely delivery:

- **Onboarding process redesign** — while closely related, onboarding will be addressed in a separate parallel workstream.
- **Involuntary reduction-in-force (RIF) or layoff event planning** — large-scale workforce reduction events have unique legal, union, and communication requirements that will be handled under a dedicated change management effort.
- **HRIS platform selection or replacement** — this initiative will work within or alongside existing systems; any platform evaluation is a separate capital project.
- **Labor relations or union contract negotiations** — changes affecting union-represented employees will be coordinated with Labor Relations but are governed by existing collective bargaining agreements.
- **Contractor or vendor offboarding** — third-party workforce offboarding involves separate procurement and legal processes and will be scoped independently.
- **Recruitment or backfill process design** — filling vacated roles is managed through Talent Acquisition and is outside the boundary of this effort.
